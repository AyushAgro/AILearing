About This Volume

Objective

  • This volume is the index layer of the book: it does not teach new systems, and it does not ship a new forge package.
  • Collect research papers into thematic and chronological roadmaps with cross-references to the volumes that teach them.
  • Provide a curated set of paper cards spanning the curriculum, plus a dense catalogue of further high-impact work.
  • Define the terminology stack precisely, with volume pointers for every core term and dozens of engineering terms used throughout.
  • Bank advanced interview questions by volume, each with a short answer outline and one follow-up.
  • Consolidate the FLOPs, memory, KV-cache, throughput and cost formulas and the latency numbers every AI engineer should know by heart.

What this volume assumes

  • You have worked through (or can jump into) Volumes 1–24. Definitions here are precise summaries, not first-principles derivations.
  • You use this volume as a lookup: before an interview, when reading a new paper, or when reviewing capacity numbers.

How to use this reference

  • Chapters 1–2: orient yourself in the research literature by theme or by year.
  • Chapter 3: deepen on a paper; jump to the teaching volume for implementation.
  • Chapter 4: settle terminology disputes (agent versus workflow, harness versus framework).
  • Chapter 5: rehearse interview answers volume by volume.
  • Chapter 6: check arithmetic before writing a design doc or a capacity plan.
Index vs encyclopaedia
  • An encyclopaedia tries to be complete. This volume is an index: dense pointers, curated cards, and arithmetic you reuse.
  • If a topic needs a derivation, leave Volume 25 and open the teaching volume listed in the table.
  • If a topic needs a production implementation, open the corresponding forge.* package rather than copying card prose into code.

Theme Notes for Practitioners

Transformers → architectures

  • If you implement one thing from Volume 2, make it causal MHA with tests against a reference.
  • If you ship one thing from Volume 7, make it GQA-aware KV sizing — it changes hardware buy decisions.
  • FlashAttention is a kernel/systems paper with model-facing consequences: longer practical context in training.

Pretraining → scaling

  • Data quality compounds: a 10% filter bug can waste more money than a 10% MFU gap.
  • Kaplan → Chinchilla → inference-aware scaling is the planning arc; do not cite 2020 constants blindly in 2026.
  • 6ND remains the shared language for “how big was this train?” even when wall-clock disagrees.

Alignment → efficiency

  • Post-training is where most product behaviour is decided; pretraining sets the ceiling.
  • DPO simplified open alignment; RL with verifiers returned for reasoning (Volume 9).
  • Serving papers (Orca, vLLM) matter as much as model papers once you have users.

Retrieval → agents → multimodal → reasoning

  • RAG failures are usually retrieval/context, not “the LLM is dumb”.
  • Agents without harnesses are demos; harnesses without evals are incidents waiting to happen.
  • Multimodal inherits every text problem plus grounding and perceptual attacks.
  • Reasoning models move spend from training FLOPs to test-time FLOPs — routers must know.

Dependency Patterns Worth Internalising

  1. Architecture before scale: Transformer (2017) precedes GPT-3 (2020). You cannot “scale” an unparallelisable idea cheaply.
  2. Scale before alignment: GPT-3-style bases precede InstructGPT. Alignment needs a capable prior.
  3. Alignment before agents: Tool loops on unaligned bases amplify harm; Instruct/RLHF unlocked safe-ish tool use.
  4. Serving before ubiquity: Continuous batching and paged KV turned demos into affordable products.
  5. Verifiers before reasoning RL: Unit tests and PRMs make RL on long CoT workable.

Full Cards — Foundations and Scale

RESEARCH PAPER Attention Is All You Need Vaswani et al., 2017 · NeurIPS · arXiv:1706.03762 · Vol 2
Problem
RNN/CNN sequence models train slowly and struggle with long-range dependencies.
Previous limitation
Recurrence serialises time; convolutions need depth for long receptive fields.
Approach
Replace recurrence with multi-head self-attention plus positional encodings; encoder–decoder stack.
Contribution
The Transformer: fully parallel training, strong translation quality, the template for modern LMs.
Practical impact
Became the default architecture for LLMs, VLMs and many speech models.
Limitations
Quadratic attention in sequence length; absolute positions age poorly (later fixed by RoPE and relatives).
RESEARCH PAPER Language Models are Few-Shot Learners Brown et al., 2020 · NeurIPS · arXiv:2005.14165 · Vol 1, 6
Problem
Every NLP task still needed labelled fine-tuning data and a separate training run.
Previous limitation
Pretrained LMs transferred, but usable behaviour still required task heads or fine-tuning.
Approach
Scale a decoder-only LM (GPT-3) and specify tasks in prompts with zero/few-shot examples.
Contribution
In-context learning as an engineering interface: behaviour change without weight updates.
Practical impact
API-first products, prompt engineering and evaluation sets became the default prototype path.
Limitations
Prompt sensitivity; contamination; cost; base models are not aligned assistants until post-training.
RESEARCH PAPER Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models Kaplan et al., 2020 · arXiv:2001.08361 · Vol 1, 6
Problem
Labs lacked a quantitative plan for how big to make models versus how much data to train on.
Previous limitation
Architecture search and “bigger is better” heuristics without FLOP-budget geometry.
Approach
Fit power-law relationships between cross-entropy loss and parameters, data and compute.
Contribution
Predictable scaling curves that justify 6ND-style capacity planning.
Practical impact
Made compute allocation a first-class design decision for pretraining.
Limitations
Underestimated optimal tokens/parameter (revised by Chinchilla); ignores inference cost.
RESEARCH PAPER Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (Chinchilla) Hoffmann et al., 2022 · NeurIPS · arXiv:2203.15556 · Vol 6
Problem
Many large models were under-trained on tokens relative to their parameter count.
Previous limitation
Kaplan-style guidance favoured parameters over data for a fixed FLOP budget.
Approach
IsoFLOP experiments; fit compute-optimal N and D; train Chinchilla 70B on 1.4T tokens.
Contribution
~20 tokens per parameter as a practical compute-optimal rule of thumb of that era.
Practical impact
Open recipes (Llama, Mistral) shifted toward smaller models with more tokens.
Limitations
Ignores repeated-data regimes and inference-heavy deployments (later “beyond Chinchilla” work).
RESEARCH PAPER On the Opportunities and Risks of Foundation Models Bommasani et al., 2021 · arXiv:2108.07258 · Vol 1
Problem
Homogeneous large models were emerging without shared vocabulary or risk analysis.
Previous limitation
Task-specific ML surveys missed systemic effects of one model adapted many ways.
Approach
Define “foundation model”; survey capabilities, adaptation modes and societal risks.
Contribution
The framing used throughout this book: one pretrained model, many downstream systems.
Practical impact
Shaped how labs and products discuss general models and shared infrastructure.
Limitations
Report, not a method paper; some emergence claims needed later empirical checks.

Full Cards — Alignment, Retrieval and Serving

RESEARCH PAPER Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback Ouyang et al., 2022 · NeurIPS · arXiv:2203.02155 · Vol 8
Problem
Base LMs predict web text; they do not reliably follow user instructions or stay helpful/harmless.
Previous limitation
Fine-tuning on demonstrations alone underperformed human preference rankings.
Approach
SFT on instructions, train a reward model on rankings, optimise the policy with RL (PPO).
Contribution
The InstructGPT recipe that became the assistant default.
Practical impact
Enabled ChatGPT-class products; defined the SFT → RM → RLHF pipeline.
Limitations
Expensive human data; reward hacking; over-refusal and sycophancy risks.
RESEARCH PAPER Direct Preference Optimization Rafailov et al., 2023 · NeurIPS · arXiv:2305.18290 · Vol 8
Problem
RLHF’s RM + RL loop is unstable, complex and costly to operate.
Previous limitation
Preference learning required a separate reward model and on-policy RL.
Approach
Reparameterise the RLHF objective so the policy is trained directly on preference pairs.
Contribution
A simple classification-style loss that matches or beats PPO-RLHF on many chat tasks.
Practical impact
Default open-source alignment path; many variants (IPO, KTO, ORPO) followed.
Limitations
Sensitive to preference data quality; not a full substitute for online RL in all regimes.
RESEARCH PAPER LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models Hu et al., 2021 · ICLR · arXiv:2106.09685 · Vol 8
Problem
Full fine-tuning of large models is memory-heavy and multiplies checkpoint storage per task.
Previous limitation
Adapters and prefix methods still added latency or underperformed full FT in some settings.
Approach
Freeze base weights; inject trainable low-rank matrices into attention (and later MLP) projections.
Contribution
Rank-r updates that match full FT quality with orders-of-magnitude fewer trainable params.
Practical impact
Industry-default PEFT; composes with quantisation (QLoRA).
Limitations
Rank and target-module choices matter; multi-adapter serving needs routing or merging strategy.
RESEARCH PAPER Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Lewis et al., 2020 · NeurIPS · arXiv:2005.11401 · Vol 14
Problem
Parametric LMs hallucinate and go stale on knowledge-intensive tasks.
Previous limitation
Fine-tuning cannot cheaply refresh facts; pure retrieval lacks fluent generation.
Approach
Retrieve documents with a neural retriever; condition a seq2seq generator on them (RAG-seq / RAG-tok).
Contribution
Joint parametric + non-parametric memory as a first-class architecture.
Practical impact
Template for enterprise assistants, support bots and research copilots.
Limitations
Retrieval errors dominate; chunking, freshness and citation faithfulness remain hard.
RESEARCH PAPER Efficient Memory Management for Large Language Model Serving with PagedAttention Kwon et al., 2023 · SOSP · arXiv:2309.06180 · Vol 11, 12
Problem
KV-cache memory is fragmented and over-reserved, limiting batch size and throughput.
Previous limitation
Contiguous per-request KV allocation wastes GPU memory under variable lengths.
Approach
Store KV in fixed-size blocks (pages); map logical tokens to physical blocks like virtual memory.
Contribution
PagedAttention + continuous batching in vLLM with large throughput gains.
Practical impact
De-facto open serving stack pattern; inspired SGLang, TensorRT-LLM features and more.
Limitations
Block size and scheduling still need tuning; prefix caching and multi-LoRA add complexity.

Full Cards — Reasoning, Agents and Multimodal

RESEARCH PAPER Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models Wei et al., 2022 · NeurIPS · arXiv:2201.11903 · Vol 9, 13
Problem
Standard few-shot prompting fails on multi-step arithmetic, logic and symbolic tasks.
Previous limitation
Models jumped to answers without allocating tokens to intermediate computation.
Approach
Demonstrate exemplars that include explicit reasoning steps before the final answer.
Contribution
Showed that intermediate tokens dramatically improve multi-step performance at scale.
Practical impact
Foundation for self-consistency, ToT, process rewards and reasoning-model training.
Limitations
Verbose and costly; unfaithful rationales; weaker models gain little.
RESEARCH PAPER ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models Yao et al., 2022 · ICLR 2023 · arXiv:2210.03629 · Vol 15
Problem
Pure reasoning traces lack grounding; pure action agents lack deliberation.
Previous limitation
CoT does not query tools; tool-only loops do not plan in language.
Approach
Interleave “Thought / Action / Observation” steps in one prompt loop.
Contribution
A simple pattern for tool-using agents that remains the mental model for many products.
Practical impact
Inspired LangChain-style agents, research assistants and coding agent scaffolds.
Limitations
Prompt-brittle; no hard permissions/budgets — needs a harness for production (Vol 16).
RESEARCH PAPER Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision Radford et al., 2021 · ICML · arXiv:2103.00020 · Vol 19
Problem
Vision models trained on fixed label sets do not transfer zero-shot to new categories.
Previous limitation
Supervised ImageNet-style training does not use free-form image–text supervision.
Approach
Contrastive training of image and text encoders on 400M web pairs (CLIP).
Contribution
A shared embedding space enabling zero-shot classification and retrieval.
Practical impact
Backbone for VLMs, multimodal RAG, moderation and generative conditioning.
Limitations
Bag/compositional blind spots; web-data biases; not a generative model by itself.
RESEARCH PAPER High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models Rombach et al., 2022 · CVPR · arXiv:2112.10752 · Vol 20
Problem
Pixel-space diffusion is too expensive for high-resolution images.
Previous limitation
DDPMs worked but scaled poorly in compute and memory with resolution.
Approach
Train a diffusion model in the latent space of a pretrained autoencoder (LDM).
Contribution
Practical high-res synthesis with accessible compute; basis of Stable Diffusion.
Practical impact
Democratised text-to-image; template for latent video and audio diffusion.
Limitations
Autoencoder artefacts; prompt adherence and compositionality still research-active.
RESEARCH PAPER Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems Sculley et al., 2015 · NeurIPS · Vol 1, 22
Problem
ML systems are easy to prototype but expensive to maintain in production.
Previous limitation
Classical software debt concepts ignored data dependencies and feedback loops.
Approach
Catalogue ML-specific debt: entanglement, hidden feedback, undeclared consumers, glue code.
Contribution
The diagram of ML code as a tiny box inside a large system.
Practical impact
Shaped MLOps; for LLMs, prompts, indexes and tools are the new debt sources.
Limitations
Qualitative; predates foundation models and agent harnesses.
RESEARCH PAPER FlashAttention: Fast and Memory-Efficient Exact Attention Dao et al., 2022 · NeurIPS · arXiv:2205.14135 · Vol 7, 11
Problem
Standard attention materialises S×S matrices, exhausting GPU HBM and slowing training/prefill.
Previous limitation
Approximate attention traded quality; naive exact attention was IO-bound.
Approach
Tiling + online softmax to keep softmax statistics in SRAM; exact attention with fewer HBM moves.
Contribution
Wall-clock and memory wins for exact attention; foundation for FA2/FA3.
Practical impact
Default kernel path in modern training and many serving stacks for long prefill.
Limitations
Helps decode less when weight/KV bandwidth dominates; hardware-specific kernels.
RESEARCH PAPER GQA: Training Generalized Multi-Query Transformer Models Ainslie et al., 2023 · arXiv:2305.13245 · Vol 7, 11
Problem
MHA KV caches are too large for high-concurrency serving; MQA can hurt quality.
Previous limitation
Binary choice between costly MHA and aggressive MQA.
Approach
Group query heads to share KV heads; uptrain/convert checkpoints to GQA.
Contribution
Practical Pareto frontier between quality and KV bandwidth/memory.
Practical impact
Adopted by Llama 2/3-class open models and many production stacks.
Limitations
Group size is a hyperparameter; conversion needs careful continued training.
RESEARCH PAPER Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts Liu et al., 2023 · arXiv:2307.03172 · Vol 13, 14
Problem
Models with large context windows still fail to use evidence placed mid-prompt.
Previous limitation
Marketing “NIk context” as uniformly usable memory.
Approach
Controlled multi-document QA varying needle position; measure accuracy vs position.
Contribution
U-shaped position curve: primacy and recency beat the middle.
Practical impact
Context engineering: put critical evidence at edges; rerank; cite; evaluate placement.
Limitations
Task-specific; newer long-context training partially mitigates but does not erase the issue.
RESEARCH PAPER Let’s Verify Step by Step Lightman et al., 2023 · arXiv:2305.20050 · Vol 9
Problem
Outcome-only rewards give poor credit assignment on multi-step maths.
Previous limitation
Final-answer RL struggles to train reliable intermediate reasoning.
Approach
Collect process supervision on steps; train process reward models; search with PRMs.
Contribution
Process supervision outperforms outcome supervision on MATH under matched labels.
Practical impact
Template for verifier-guided decoding and reasoning-model training.
Limitations
Expensive human process labels; domain-limited; unfaithful steps still possible.
RESEARCH PAPER Speculative Decoding Leviathan et al., 2023 · ICML · arXiv:2211.17192 · Vol 11
Problem
Autoregressive decode of large models is memory-bound and slow at low batch.
Previous limitation
Faster approximations changed the output distribution.
Approach
Small draft model proposes γ tokens; large model verifies in one forward; accept/reject keeps exact dist.
Contribution
Lossless speedups when draft acceptance is high.
Practical impact
Widely deployed (Medusa, EAGLE, provider speculation features).
Limitations
Low acceptance wastes work; draft must match domain; implementation complexity.
RESEARCH PAPER QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs Dettmers et al., 2023 · NeurIPS · arXiv:2305.14314 · Vol 8
Problem
Finetuning 65B-class models needed multi-GPU memory beyond many practitioners.
Previous limitation
LoRA still kept base weights in high precision in memory.
Approach
4-bit NormalFloat base + LoRA adapters + paged optimisers.
Contribution
Finetune very large models on a single 48GB GPU-class machine.
Practical impact
Democratised instruction/domain adapters; default hobbyist/pro PEFT path.
Limitations
Quant noise; not always equal to full FT; serving still needs adapter strategy.

Common misuse (counter-examples)

  • Calling a single Chat Completions call an “agent” — that is a model call inside an application or workflow.

  • Equating “foundation model” with “any neural net” — FMs are broad, adaptable, scale-trained bases.

Notation
  • N — non-embedding parameter count (approx. total params for large models).
  • D — training tokens. S — sequence / context length. B — batch (sequences).

Worked Numerical Examples

Example A — Training FLOPs to GPU-hours

  • N = 70B, D = 1.4T (Chinchilla-like), FLOPs ≈ 6ND = 6 × 7e10 × 1.4e12 = 5.88e23.
  • H100 peak ≈ 9.89e14 FLOP/s; at MFU 0.4 with 1024 GPUs: useful = 0.4 × 9.89e14 × 1024.
  • Seconds ≈ 5.88e23 / useful ≈ 1.45e6 s ≈ 400 GPU-hours × 1024 ≈ 0.4M GPU-hours order — then inflate for real MFU/failures.

Example B — KV concurrency on 80GB

  • Weights BF16 70B ≈ 140 GB → needs multi-GPU; for 8B weights 16 GB on 80 GB leaves ~60 GB KV.
  • At 128 KiB/token, 60 GB ≈ 4.8e5 tokens of KV ⇒ e.g. ~58 sequences at 8k, or ~14 at 32k.
  • INT8 KV halves bytes/token → roughly 2× concurrency if quality allows.

Example C — API bill

  • 1M requests/day, 800 input + 200 output tokens, $0.50 / $1.50 per 1M in/out.
  • Daily $ ≈ 1e6 × (800×0.5 + 200×1.5) / 1e6 = 1e6 × (0.0004 + 0.0003) = $700/day.
  • Cut input 25% via prompt compression/caching → save $200/day before quality checks.

Example D — TTFT vs E2E

  • TTFT 0.6s, TPOT 30 ms, 150 output tokens → E2E ≈ 0.6 + 149×0.03 ≈ 5.1s.
  • Halving output length saves ~2.2s; halving TTFT saves 0.3s — pick the bottleneck by product.

Example E — pass@k intuition

  • If pass@1 = 0.2 independent, pass@k ≈ 1 − (1−0.2)^k → pass@5 ≈ 0.67, pass@10 ≈ 0.89.
  • Real samples are correlated; measure empirically; cost scales with k.

  • Planning with peak FLOP/s and 100% MFU — understates time/cost by 2–5×.

Inequalities You Will Use Constantly

  • M_{\text{KV}} \propto B \cdot S \cdot h_{kv} — cut any factor to raise concurrency.
  • \text{TTFT} \ge t_{\text{queue}} + t_{\text{prefill}} — you cannot fix queueing with a faster kernel alone.
  • \$_{\text{task}} \ge \$_{\text{tokens}} + \$_{\text{tools}} + \$_{\text{humans}} — token bills understate agentic products.
  • \text{goodput} \le \text{throughput} — equality only if nothing fails SLOs.
  • \text{quality}(\text{test-time compute}) saturates — measure the knee before spending.
Whiteboard checklist

  • Cost = $/s ÷ useful tokens/s; optimise goodput not vanity TPS.
