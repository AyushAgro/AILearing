Why Synthetic Data

Synthetic data fills holes that crawls and logs cannot: rare failure modes, privacy-sensitive domains and capabilities that barely appear in the wild. It cannot invent ground truth the generator does not have, and it can quietly poison the evals that were meant to measure progress.

The Engineering Problem

Your support assistant fails on refund-edge cases that appear once a week in production. Labelling enough real tickets would take a quarter and would put PII into the training path. A strong teacher model can invent plausible refund dialogues overnight — but some of them will paraphrase your golden eval items, some will invent illegal policies, and all of them will concentrate on the teacher’s favourite phrasings. You need a factory with gates, not a weekend of prompting.

What Synthetic Data Cannot Invent

  • New world facts the teacher never knew. Distillation copies the teacher’s knowledge ceiling; it does not expand it.
  • Ground-truth labels for subjective or policy-laden tasks without a verifier or human. A fluent wrong answer is still wrong.
  • Distributional tails the generator rarely samples. Unchecked recursion shrinks support (Chapter 12; Shumailov et al.).
  • Licence cleanliness by rewriting. Paraphrase is not a copyright solvent; record generator terms in lineage (Chapter 11).
Key Idea
  • Synthetic data is a multiplier on seed quality and verifier strength, not a substitute for either.
  • Every factory that can improve a training set can also contaminate an eval set. Treat contamination control as a first-class stage, not a pre-launch scramble.

The Factory Contract

Volume 5 treated synthetic text as one stage in a crawl pipeline. This volume promotes it to a product with an explicit contract:

  1. Inputs: seeds, taxonomy, optional retrieval corpus, frozen eval digests.
  2. Process: generate → filter → dedup → decontaminate → package.
  3. Outputs: versioned shards, lineage manifest, release-gate report.
  4. Non-goals: replacing real data entirely; claiming “eval-safe” without a measured overlap threshold.

  • Generating “until the count looks big” with no taxonomy or coverage metric.
  • Training on synthetic data that was never checked against the eval suite it will be judged on.
  • Assuming privacy because the text is “made up” — memorised PII still appears.

Evidence Labels for Synthetic Claims

  • When a deck says “synthetic data improved X by Y%”, label the claim Documented only if you have a controlled ablation with a frozen eval digest; otherwise mark it Inferred.
  • Never cite factory row counts as quality evidence. Counts are capacity, not competence.
  • Volume 27 FDEs inherit this discipline: PoC metrics on synthetic corpora stay Inferred until real-doc pilots confirm them.

Generator Designs

Generators turn seeds into candidates. The design choice is less “which model” than “what structure forces diversity and what verifier can reject junk”.

The Engineering Problem

A free-form “write 10,000 support dialogues” prompt collapses into a handful of templates with different adjectives. You need controllable axes (topic, audience, format, difficulty), optional retrieval grounding, and a place to attach verifiers before anything hits the filter stack.

Prompt Templates and Grids

  • Template: a string with named slots filled from a taxonomy.
  • Grid expansion: Cartesian product of axes; sample if the product is huge.
  • Persona / style slots: change surface form without changing the task.

Multi-Agent Debate (Lightweight)

  • Pattern: proposer → critic → reviser. Keep only the revised answer if the critic’s checklist passes.
  • Useful when single-shot answers omit constraints (citations, refusals, schema).
  • Cost multiplies by the number of roles; budget it like a filter, not free quality.
issues: ['missing_policy_pointer']
final: Draft answer for: Explain how to escalate a missed SLA. Steps: 1) identify 2) act 3) confirm. | revised_for=missing_policy_pointer; cite policy handbook §4.

Retrieval-Augmented Synthesis

  • Retrieve real (or ACL-safe) passages; condition generation on them; store source_ids in metadata.
  • Reduces hallucination of policy text; introduces retrieval failure modes (wrong doc, stale doc).
  • For FDE PoCs, prefer synthetic docs that never touch customer text (Chapter 9).

  • Later runs: open hard cells (adversarial, multi-constraint) once verifiers exist.
  • A curriculum is a versioned schedule object in the manifest, not an oral tradition.

Quality Filters

Generators over-produce. Filters decide what is allowed to train — and they introduce their own biases. Stack cheap heuristics before expensive judges, and never skip human spot checks on a stratified sample.

The Engineering Problem

A factory emits 50k candidates overnight. Ten percent are empty, duplicated, or placeholder prose; another slice is fluent but wrong; a judge model loves long answers. You need a layered stack with measurable keep rates and a budgeted human review queue.

Heuristic Filters

  • Length bounds, language ID, placeholder regexes, repetition ratios, banned substrings.
  • Return a reason code for every drop — histograms drive threshold tuning (Volume 5 habit).
{'too_short': 1, 'verbosity_bait': 1, 'ok': 1, 'placeholder': 1}
kept 1 of 4

Model Judges and Cross-Model Agreement

  • Pointwise rubric scores (Volume 10) are useful as soft filters: keep if score ≥ threshold.
  • Cross-model agreement: two independent judges (or one judge + verifier) must agree before keep. Disagreement goes to quarantine or human review.
  • Pin judge model versions; track drift on an anchor set (Volume 10).
Cite policy §2; confirm timeline.... -> (True, 'agree:0.90/0.85')
Certainly! more words more words more wor... -> (False, 'disagree:0.40/0.45')

Human Spot Checks

  • Sample stratified by taxonomy cell and by filter decision (kept vs borderline).
  • Measure precision of automatic keeps and recall of automatic drops on the sample.
  • Budget: a fixed number of reviews per factory release, not “when we have time”.

Aggressive automatic filters

  • Cheap at scale; remove obvious junk early.
  • Reason codes make regressions debuggable.

Costs

  • Heuristics over-delete dialects, code and maths.
  • Judges encode verbosity and self-preference biases.
Key Idea
  • Order filters by cost: heuristic → verifier → judge → human.
  • Every automatic keep that humans would reject is a training poison; every automatic drop that humans would keep is a coverage hole. Measure both.

Diversity and Dedup

Synthetic factories love to repeat themselves. Near-duplicate detection, topic coverage metrics and taxonomy-driven sampling keep the shard from being one prompt in a thousand costumes.

The Engineering Problem

After Self-Instruct-style expansion you have 8k rows, but MinHash says 40% are near-duplicates of the seed pool, and three taxonomy cells have zero examples. Training will look like it saw more data than it did.

Near-Duplicate Detection with MinHash

Reuse the Volume 5 estimator: shingles → MinHash signatures → Jaccard estimate. For factories, greedy keep-if-below-threshold is enough at modest scale; LSH banding helps at large scale.

Topic Coverage and Taxonomy Sampling

  • Maintain a coverage table: count per taxonomy cell after dedup.
  • Upsample empty or thin cells; cap cells that dominate.
  • Report coverage entropy: if a few cells own most mass, the factory failed its brief.

Contamination Control

A synthetic shard that overlaps a frozen eval set buys fake progress. n-gram and embedding overlap checks, plus quarantine workflows, are mandatory release stages for any eval-safe factory.

The Engineering Problem

Your instruction factory was seeded from internal wiki text that also supplied the golden set. Overnight generation paraphrases three eval items into training. Offline scores jump; production does not. Without an overlap gate you will ship the illusion.

n-gram Overlap with Held-Out Evals

Index the frozen eval set; score each candidate by the fraction of the eval item’s n-grams found in the candidate (Volume 5 / Volume 10). Quarantine if any item exceeds \tau.

\mathrm{overlap}(q, d) = \frac{|G_n(q) \cap G_n(d)|}{|G_n(q)|}
clean=2 quarantined=1
  doc[1] overlap=1.00 hit=e1

Embedding Overlap (Complement)

  • n-grams miss paraphrase. Embedding nearest-neighbour against eval items catches many of those cases (approximate; needs a threshold calibrated on known paraphrases).
  • Combine: long n-gram pass + embedding pass + behavioural canaries (Volume 5).

Quarantine Workflow

  1. Automatic quarantine when overlap ≥ \tau.
  2. Human (or dual-judge) review for the borderline band [\tau/2, \tau).
  3. Never silently drop without counting — quarantine rate is a factory health metric.
  4. Frozen eval digests are inputs to the release gate (Chapter 11). Changing the eval set changes the digest and invalidates prior “clean” claims.
Customer evals
  • For FDE engagements (Volume 27), the customer’s golden set is co-owned and often confidential. Run overlap checks inside their boundary; do not ship their items into your public teacher prompts.
  • Decontaminating only public benchmarks while private golden sets leak into seeds.
  • Setting \tau so high that only verbatim copies are caught.
  • Re-using quarantined text after light paraphrase without re-checking.

Canaries in Synthetic Shards

  • Inject unique canary strings into a held-out eval slice (Volume 5 pattern).
  • After training, measure canary completion rate; non-zero rates indicate leakage even when n-gram gates looked clean on other items.
  • Factories should refuse to generate when the seed pool itself contains canary markers — that is organisational contamination, not model failure.

SFT and Instruction Factories

Self-Instruct-style pipelines turn a small seed pool into a large instruction set. Licence tags and PII scrubbing must ride along; otherwise you scale risk as efficiently as you scale data.

The Engineering Problem

You have 200 high-quality seed tasks. You need ~1k filtered instruction–response pairs for SFT, with proof that overlap against a frozen eval is below \tau, and a manifest that records the teacher model and seed.

Self-Instruct Pattern

  • Wang et al. (2022): bootstrap instructions from seeds; generate responses; filter.
  • Engineering additions: taxonomy slots, verifiers, dedup, eval overlap, PII, licence.
  • phi / textbook-quality work (Gunasekar et al. 2023) stresses that quality and structure can beat raw volume — filters are not optional polish.
reasons: {'ok': 30, 'exact_dup': 16}
shipped: 14
sample license: synthetic:scripted-teacher@demo

PII and licence
  • Scrub after generation; teachers invent emails and phone patterns.
  • Tag every row with license_tag / generator terms; mixture samplers must be able to exclude restricted tags.

The practical project at the end of this volume scales this loop to ~1k pairs with MinHash dedup, a stricter \tau, and a full lineage manifest.

Scaling the Loop to ~1k Pairs

  • Batch generation by taxonomy cell; checkpoint partial shards with interim manifests.
  • Run MinHash dedup globally at the end — per-cell dedup misses cross-cell clones.
  • Keep a rejected/ tree keyed by reason code for audits; do not discard silently.
  • Budget teacher tokens explicitly: tokens per kept row is a first-class KPI.

Large synthetic SFT sets

  • Cover long-tail instructions cheaply.
  • Stabilise format following before preference optimisation.

Costs

  • Homogenised style; hidden eval leaks; licence exposure.
  • Easy to overfit judges that look like the teacher.

Preference and Critique Data

Pairwise preference data trains reward models and DPO-style objectives. RLAIF critiques scale labelling — and scale length bias, sycophancy and self-preference unless you design traps and audits.

The Engineering Problem

Humans cannot label 100k pairs. An LLM produces chosen/rejected pairs overnight. The reward model learns “longer is better” and “agree with the user”. Offline preference accuracy rises; helpfulness in production falls.

Pairwise Generation

  • Sample two responses (or response + deliberate degradation); ask a judge which is better under a rubric; store (x, y_w, y_l).
  • Swap order when using pairwise judges (Volume 10); drop ties.
  • Keep provenance: judge id, rubric version, temperatures.
chosen_starts: Certainly! I would be delighted to help you today.
flags: ['length_bias_trap']
rejected_has_old_pw: False
  • The biased judge picked the long unsafe answer. The length_bias_trap flag lets you measure how often this happens and filter or re-label those pairs.
  • A safer builder prefers a verifier (policy keywords, safety rules) before the soft judge, or uses an ensemble that must agree.

RLAIF-Style Critiques

  • Bai et al. (2022): constitutional principles → critiques → revisions.
  • Store critique text for audit; do not keep only the final revision.
  • Principles must be versioned like rubrics; silent principle edits change the data distribution.
Key Idea
  • Preference factories need adversarial traps (length, sycophancy, unsafe but polite) with known labels, the same way eval suites need canaries.
  • Report trap catch-rate next to pair count on every release.

Reasoning Trace Factories

Verified maths and code traces are the gold standard of synthetic reasoning data — when the trace is faithful to the check, not a post-hoc story attached to a lucky answer.

The Engineering Problem

You generate chain-of-thought for arithmetic and keep rows whose final answer matches. Many traces are nonsense that ends with the right number. A student model learns fluent unfaithful reasoning and fails when intermediate steps are audited.

Verified Traces and Rejection Sampling

  • Generate (x, \text{trace}, \hat{y}); accept only if a verifier confirms \hat{y} (and optionally intermediate checks).
  • Rejection sampling: repeat until accept or budget exhausted; track accept rate.
  • For code: run tests; keep trace + programme only on pass.

  • Report accept rate and faithful rate on every trace shard.
Faithfulness risk
  • Answer-only filters create rationalisation datasets.
  • For high-stakes domains, prefer domains with executable intermediates (unit tests, symbolic proofs) over prose CoT.

RAG and Domain Corpora

Synthetic documents can pad a RAG corpus for demos and privacy-safe PoCs. They are not a drop-in replacement for real source-of-truth docs, and they must respect ACL boundaries.

The Engineering Problem

An FDE needs a PoC retriever on Monday. The customer cannot export Confluence. A teacher invents a plausible handbook. The demo sings; the pilot on real docs fails because the synthetic corpus never contained the weird legacy policy names that drive real queries.

Eval Set Factories

Factories can also build evaluation sets: adversarial and stratified items, versioned and co-owned with customers. The same contamination discipline applies in reverse — training factories must not eat eval factories.

The Engineering Problem

Your golden set is 80 easy paraphrases of happy-path tickets. Models saturate it. Production still fails on hostile users, empty fields and policy conflicts. You need a stratified, versioned eval factory with an adversarial slice — and a hard wall against using those items in training.

Adversarial and Stratified Sets

  • Strata: intent, severity, language, customer tier, refusal vs answer.
  • Adversarial: prompt injection, missing slots, contradictory policy, out-of-ACL retrieval asks.
  • Generate candidates → human or dual review → freeze → digest.
n= 12 digest= 1631ee3c40def7ef
strata: {'happy': 4, 'missing_slot': 4, 'adversarial': 4}

Versioning and Customer Co-Ownership

  • Eval sets are immutable versions in a registry (Volume 10). Edits create a new version.
  • Co-ownership (Volume 27): customer approves labels; both sides store the digest.
  • Training factories take the eval digest as an input to the overlap gate — never the reverse.
Key Idea
  • Separate namespaces: train/* and eval/* with different access controls.
  • A single shared “data lake” folder is how contamination happens organisationally.

Governance and Lineage

If you cannot say which seed, teacher, filter versions and eval digest produced a shard, you cannot audit contamination, honour licence constraints or reproduce a release. Manifests are the product.

The Engineering Problem

Six months later a benchmark looks contaminated. Nobody can tell whether factory run instruction_v3 used eval digest A or B, or which teacher terms applied. The shard is radioactive and the lineage is folklore.

Lineage Manifest

  • Content-addressed shard digest (Volume 5).
  • Seeds hash, generator model + version, filter config, \tau, eval digest, timestamps, operator.
  • Release gate: refuse to publish without a complete manifest and passing checks.

Failure Modes

Synthetic factories fail in characteristic ways: model collapse, echo chambers, benchmark hacking and licence laundering. Name them so gates can look for them.

The Engineering Problem

Each generation of models trains a little more on the last generation’s outputs. Tails die, styles converge, and “new” eval items look oddly familiar because the factory and the benchmark share a teacher. Leadership sees rising curves; the distribution is shrinking.

Model Collapse

Shumailov et al. showed recursive training on model outputs erodes distributional tails. Mitigations: retain real data, accumulate rather than replace, track support/entropy proxies, diversify teachers.

Echo Chambers and Benchmark Hacking

  • Echo chamber: teacher, judge and critic are the same model family; errors reinforce.
  • Benchmark hacking: factories conditioned on eval-like stems until overlap gates are the only defence — and someone raises \tau to “unblock” a release.
  • Mitigation: independent judge family; frozen eval digests; change control on \tau.

Licence Laundering

  • Rewriting copyrighted or restrictively licensed text and calling it “synthetic” does not clear rights.
  • Stripping license_tag / generator metadata is an integrity failure, not a cleanup.
  • Release gates must require tags; mixture samplers must honour deny-lists.

Volume Review

What You Learned

  • Synthetic data multiplies seed quality and verifier strength; it cannot invent unseen facts or replace contamination control.
  • Generator designs that matter: taxonomy grids, debate/revise loops, retrieval-augmented synthesis and early verifiers.
  • Filter stacks should be ordered by cost; reason codes and human spot checks keep them honest.
  • Diversity needs MinHash near-dedup plus coverage entropy over the taxonomy.
  • Eval overlap with threshold \tau, quarantine workflows and frozen eval digests are mandatory for eval-safe factories.
  • Self-Instruct-style SFT factories must carry licence tags and PII scrubbing.
  • Preference/critique data needs length-bias and safety traps; RLAIF principles are versioned artefacts.
  • Reasoning-trace factories need faithfulness checks, not only final-answer match.
  • Synthetic RAG corpora are for ACL-safe demos and privacy, not production truth.
  • Eval set factories are stratified and versioned; train and eval namespaces stay separate.
  • Lineage manifests and release gates make synthetic data auditable.
  • Collapse, echo chambers, benchmark hacking and licence laundering are first-class failure modes.

What You Built

  • forge.synth generators: grid expansion, debate stub, ACL-safe corpus docs.
  • Heuristic + dual-judge filter stack with reason codes.
  • MinHash greedy dedup and taxonomy coverage reports.
  • Eval-overlap index with quarantine (aligned with forge.data / forge.eval).

Failure Modes

  • Row-count KPIs without coverage, overlap or trap metrics.
  • Missing eval-overlap stage; τ raised under schedule pressure.
  • Teacher–judge echo chambers; uncalibrated LLM filters.
  • Answer-only acceptance of reasoning traces.
  • Synthetic RAG corpora presented as production truth.
  • Licence metadata stripped; generator terms ignored.
  • Train/eval namespace collapse in shared storage.

Evaluation

  • Factory health: keep-rate by reason, near-dup rate, coverage entropy, quarantine rate, trap catch-rate, manifest completeness.
  • Downstream: ablations of filtered vs unfiltered shards on a separate eval digest; never tune τ on the gate’s own eval.
  • Human spot-check precision/recall on stratified samples each release.

Practical Project

Build and run an eval-safe instruction factory with forge.synth:

  1. Start from ≥ 50 seed tasks spanning a documented taxonomy (≥ 4 axes or cells).
  2. Generate candidates until you can ship about 1 000 filtered instruction–response pairs (scripted teacher allowed for the exercise).
  3. Apply heuristic filters, MinHash near-dedup, PII scrubbing and licence tags.
  4. Against a frozen eval set, prove maximum n-gram overlap < \tau (choose and justify τ; default 0.05 on 8-grams for the exercise).
  5. Emit manifest.json with seed, generator model, filters, τ, eval digest and shard digest; release_gate must pass.
  6. Write a one-page report: coverage table, reason-code histogram, quarantine count, and what synthetic data cannot claim for your domain.

Exercises

  1. Extend the grid generator with a difficulty axis; show how coverage entropy changes when you sample versus fully expand.
  2. Add an embedding-similarity quarantine stub (even with bag-of-words cosine) and compare catch-rate to 8-gram overlap on paraphrased eval leaks.
  3. Repair the biased preference judge with a policy verifier that rejects “ask for old password”; remeasure length-bias trap hits.
  4. Add an intermediate-step check to the maths factory so lucky finals with wrong steps are rejected; report the change in accept rate.
  5. Wire python -m forge.synth instruction to write real shard.jsonl + manifest.json and fail CI when the gate errors.
  6. Simulate licence laundering: strip tags and show the gate failing; then restore tags.
  7. For an FDE-style PoC, generate an ACL-safe corpus for two tenants and demonstrate a retriever filter that never returns cross-tenant hits.

Research Papers

RESEARCH PAPER Self-Instruct: Aligning Language Models with Self-Generated Instructions Wang et al., 2022 · ACL
Problem
Instruction data is expensive to write at the scale SFT needs.
Previous limitation
Human-written instruction sets were small and narrow.
Approach
Bootstrap tasks from seeds with a strong model; generate responses; filter.
Contribution
A practical recipe for synthetic instruction data that improved follow-through.
Practical impact
Pattern for Chapter 6 factories — with modern contamination and licence controls added.
Limitations
Quality inherits the teacher; diversity and factuality still need gates.
RESEARCH PAPER Textbooks Are All You Need Gunasekar et al., 2023 · Microsoft (phi)
Problem
Can smaller models reach strong results with carefully curated data?
Previous limitation
Scaling stories emphasised volume over textbook-like quality.
Approach
Emphasise high-quality synthetic and curated “textbook” data.
Contribution
Showed quality/structure can dominate raw web scale for some regimes.
Practical impact
Justification for strict filters and structured generators in this volume.
Limitations
Results depend on evaluation choices; not a licence to skip real data.
RESEARCH PAPER AI Models Collapse When Trained on Recursively Generated Data Shumailov et al., 2023/2024 · Nature
Problem
What happens as generated content feeds future training?
Previous limitation
Concern was anecdotal without a clear mechanism.
Approach
Theory on simple models plus experiments over generations.
Contribution
Tails vanish first; errors compound across generations.
Practical impact
Chapter 12 collapse gates; retain real data; track diversity metrics.
Limitations
Replacement setting; accumulation of real data mitigates much of the effect.
RESEARCH PAPER Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback Bai et al., 2022 · Anthropic
Problem
Human preference labelling does not scale for harmlessness.
Previous limitation
RLHF depended on costly human comparisons.
Approach
Principles (constitution) → critiques → revisions → preference models (RLAIF).
Contribution
Showed AI feedback can produce useful preference/critique data.
Practical impact
Chapter 7 critique factories; principle versioning; trap audits still required.
